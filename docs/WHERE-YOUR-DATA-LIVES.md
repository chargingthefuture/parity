# What this app stores, and where

Read this once. It is short, and it is the whole story.

The same words are in the app itself, on the Data screen under "What this app stores, in plain
words".

---

## The short version

Everything this app knows is on your phone. Nothing is sent anywhere. There is no account, no
sign-in, and no company holding a copy. Turn the phone off and that is the end of it.

---

## Where you are

The app asks your phone for your position so it can work out which stops are ahead of you and which
are across the median.

That position is used on the spot and then forgotten. It is not saved. It is not written into a
history of where you have been. It is not sent off the phone. Close the app and it is gone.

The satellite receiver only runs while you have a screen open that needs it. Switch to another app
and it switches off. That is also why it does not flatten your battery.

---

## What is saved

Two things.

**The stop list.** Public map data — rest areas, truck stops, fuel — that you loaded once. It is the
same information anyone can look up. It does not change and it says nothing about you.

**Your notes.** What you wrote about a place you stopped at, and the date you were there.

That is it. There is no third thing.

### The mark on your notes

Your notes carry a four-character mark, something like **dev-7c1a9e**.

It is four random characters, made up the first time you opened the app. It is not your name. It is
not your phone number. It is not built from anything about you or your phone, and it cannot be
traced back to either.

It has one job. When several drivers pool their notes, it lets you tell "one driver wrote six notes
about this place" apart from "six drivers all agree". Without it, one person's opinion would look
like a crowd.

You can replace it whenever you want, on the Data screen. Nothing breaks when you do.

---

## Sharing

Sharing is a file. You export your notes, and you hand the file to whoever you want — a text message,
an email, a memory stick, however you normally send a photo. Someone hands you theirs and you import
it.

You choose who gets it. You can open the file and read it first; it is plain text.

**When you import someone else's notes, nothing of yours is overwritten.** Their notes are added
alongside yours. Where you and another driver rated the same place differently, the app shows both,
with the dates, and says plainly that you disagree. It does not average you into one number.

### Why it works this way

This is on purpose, and it is a safety decision.

If all of this went to one company's service, that service would hold a list of where women stopped,
when they stopped, and how often. A list like that can be subpoenaed. It can be sold. It can be
broken into. It can be handed over by whoever owns the company next.

Because this app has no server, that list does not exist anywhere. Nobody can hand over what was
never collected.

---

## What is not here

- No account and no password.
- No advertising.
- No tracking of any kind — nothing measuring what you tap, no usage reporting, no crash reporting.
- Nothing that shares your location with anyone, live or otherwise.
- No comments, no followers, no feed, no other drivers seeing you.

None of this is switched off in a setting you have to find. It was never built.

---

## The one thing to watch

Because nothing is in the cloud, **a lost, broken or wiped phone loses your notes.**

There is no "restore from your account", because there is no account. So:

Open the Data screen and tap **Export everything** now and then. Save the file somewhere off the
phone — your own cloud drive, a laptop, an email to yourself. On an iPhone, saving it to Files →
iCloud Drive is enough; that copy gets backed up with the rest of your phone.

On a new phone: install the app, open the Data screen, tap **Import**, pick the file. Your notes come
back.

That copy is yours. It goes wherever you put it, and nowhere else.
